Date: 2026-09-26. Data: public Kalshi trade tapes and settled markets (`scripts/download_kalshi_history.py`,

## 2. Method

* **Statistic.** Every public print (block trades dropped; there were none) is a maker fill at the
  print price. Maker P&L to settlement per contract: taker bought YES at p -> p - settle; taker sold
  YES -> settle - p. Contract-weighted means.
* **Fees.** Series fee types from `GET /series/{s}` today: KXBTCD, KXBTC and KXBTC15M are all
  `quadratic` x1 (`scripts/verify_fee_schedule.py`), i.e. **no maker fee**; no scheduled or
  historical series fee change and no event fee override for these series in the downloaded
  period. The exact per-order fee path (`FeeSchedule.single_fill_fees`, balance precision
  $0.0001 from `config/kalshi.yaml`) is still applied per print; it returns 0 for `quadratic`, so
  net = gross here. (A maker-fee series would be charged 0.0175 x P(1-P) with rounding.)
* **Fill policies on a public tape** (analogues of the simulator's B/C, `docs/EXECUTION_MODEL.md`):
  * **B**: every print is our fill at that price (the average maker, front and back of queue).
  * **C**: only prints a LAST-in-queue maker would receive: the last print at a price level before
    the next same-side print within 2 s trades THROUGH that level (the level was exhausted;
    same-millisecond prints are ordered as a sweep). 10 % of KXBTCD, 6 % of KXBTC15M and 18 % of
    KXBTC prints qualify; they are the more adverse fills by construction.
* **Clusters.** One settlement event = one expiration time T = `close_time` (all series expiring
  together settle on the same BRTI average, verified in `docs/research/M1_2_SETTLEMENT_CHECK.md`).
  CIs: hull of the studentized cluster bootstrap and jackknife-t (`exp_common.cluster_mean_ci`);
  segments need >= 200 settlement events; Holm across every segment examined (one-sided, H0 net <=
  0.15c, family-wise 2.5 %); "simult. lo" is the Bonferroni simultaneous lower bound used by the
  decision rule.
* **Segments.** series x tau (T - trade time: <30 s, 30-60 s, 1-5 m, 5-10 m, 10-30 m, 30-60 m) x
  YES price bucket, x |z| bucket (distance of the nearest strike from the last BRTI print known at
  the trade, 100 ms latency, in units of the expected move to T with a causal 60-minute realized
  vol floored at 20 %/yr), and price x maker side.

## 3. Caveats (read before using any number)

* **In-sample, short.** One month of KXBTCD, two weeks of KXBTC, one week of KXBTC15M. Nothing here
  is out of sample; the half-sample split (`e0_first_*` / `e0_second_*`) cannot confirm KXBTC15M
  (all of its week falls in the second half).
* **Events are not independent.** 15-minute and hourly expirations inside one trending day share
  the BTC path. Example: KXBTC15M 5-10c "maker sold YES" is +4.30c [+2.10, +5.89] clustered by
  expiration, +4.30c [+2.78, +5.73] clustered by UTC day (8 days) — but the same week shows makers
  who sold YES winning in almost every price bucket and makers who bought YES losing: a directional
  week (BTC fell against the priced odds), not a stable edge. Day-level clusters are too few (8)
  for a decision.
* **Unconditional.** E0 averages over all makers; our queue position, latency and quote placement
  decide which of these fills we would get (policy C is a crude bound, not a model).
* **Policy C is a proxy** built only from the tape (no book depth); it can mislabel a level that
  was refilled within 2 s.
* **|z| buckets** use the CF BRTI history (multi-second holes in < 0.1 % of settlement windows);
  the 0.8 % of prints with no BRTI print within 60 s are left out of the z tables only.
